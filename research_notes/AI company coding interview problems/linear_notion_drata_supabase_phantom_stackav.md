# Coding interviews at Linear, Notion, Drata, Supabase, Phantom, Stack AV (and "Adjacent") — Nov 2025 to Nov 2026

Research date: 2026-11-12. Scope: full-stack / AI engineer loops; emphasis on multi-part problems, take-homes, pair-programming and work trials.

**Source-quality warning that applies to every section.** Glassdoor (all country domains, incl. static.glassdoor.be) returned HTTP 403 to automated fetches, and dataford.io returned HTTP 429, so Glassdoor/dataford content below comes only from search-engine snippets. No Reddit, Hacker News or Blind first-hand thread dated Nov 2025 or later was found for any of the six companies. The bulk of the "problem" material in circulation comes from undated or 2026-dated SEO prep sites (techinterview.org, techscreen.app, techprep.app, finalroundai.com, supacv.com, copilotinterview.com, prachub.com, gm.careers, designgurus.io, jobsbyculture.com). Several of these (techscreen.app, linkjob.ai, programhelp.net) sell "undetectable" AI interview assistants or live-coaching services and have a commercial incentive to describe the loops in detail; none cite dated candidate reports. Treat all problem "shapes" from them as **reconstructions / inferred**, not first-hand, unless marked otherwise.

## Key Question 1: What exact coding problems or exercises (with parts) did each company ask in the last year?

### Takeaway
Only Notion yields concrete, candidate-reported problem statements in the window (a `computeAggregations` table problem with two escalating follow-ups; a "complete the pre-stubbed functions of a text editor" screen). Linear officially has no coding test at all (paid 2–5 day work trial instead). For Supabase, Drata, Phantom and Stack AV, no first-hand coding-problem reports dated Nov 2025+ were found; what exists is third-party "typical shape" guidance of low confidence.

#### Linear (Product/Full-stack Engineer)

- Official "How we hire at Linear" (Doug Parker, **Apr 28 2026**): stages are application review → early conversations (recruiter and/or hiring manager; comp discussed on first call) → "typically three to four" team interviews → **paid work trial as the final stage for all roles** → decision/offer. Engineers' interviews "discuss coding, architecture, and design decisions". **No coding test, take-home or live-coding format is described.** Trial is 2–5 days with a small project team, access to internal tools, "All work trials are paid", daily rate shared in advance, detailed guide provided. Decision: independent written feedback, blind vote, debrief, hiring manager decides with unanimity strongly preferred. Most processes 4–6 weeks. Values: "slope over credentials"; craft, judgment, ownership, clarity. — [linear.app/now/how-we-hire-at-linear](https://linear.app/now/how-we-hire-at-linear)
- Official "Why and how we do work trials" (Dec 13 2023; **pre-window but still linked from the 2026 page**): trial is "a paid 2-5 day period", senior roles "typically take the full five days", weekends sometimes used; team defines the successful output from current priorities and works backwards to design the prompt, "limit the scope ... while getting as close to a real project as possible"; sometimes the trial project becomes the hire's first project. The one engineering example given: "building a feature allowing teams to effectively triage incoming feature requests". Project team = recruiter, HM, 3–5 teammates; candidate gets Slack, Notion, Linear, Pitch, GitHub, Figma under NDA. Structure: kickoff → candidate-led check-ins ("because we want to see their ownership and readiness to course-correct") → discussion-based final presentation. "All debriefs begin with a blind thumbs up/down vote"; "anything other than a strong yes is a no." — [linear.app/blog/why-and-how-we-do-work-trials-at-linear](https://linear.app/blog/why-and-how-we-do-work-trials-at-linear)
- Techscreen (Jun 4 2026) corroborates "Linear has no coding screen ... no algorithmic phone screen and no timed algorithm questions"; describes a 45–60 min founder/staff conversation and a 60–90 min technical-depth walkthrough of a shipped project; trial in TypeScript (React, custom WebSocket sync engine, Postgres) in "the real Linear codebase or a sandboxed mirror" with acceptance criteria; "Treat the trial as a working week, not as a take-home exercise." Uncited. — [techscreen.app Linear](https://techscreen.app/articles/linear-technical-interview-process-2026)
- **Contradiction:** techinterview.org (Apr 24 2026, updated Jul 3 2026) claims a 60-min TypeScript phone screen plus a 4–8 hour take-home ("building a small feature end-to-end (data model, client, sync) or extending a starter app") and 1–2 onsite coding rounds, and does not mention the work trial at all. This conflicts with Linear's own page and is uncited; discount it. Its "shapes": issues with parent/child relationships and cycles; sync primitive merging local/remote edits; command-palette fuzzy search; topological sort for sub-issue rollup. — [techinterview.org Linear](https://www.techinterview.org/companies/linear/)

## Key Question 2: Which problems are multi-part, and how do the parts escalate?

### Takeaway
The only candidate-reported multi-part problem is Notion's table-aggregation question, which escalates from a straightforward implementation to caching under read-heavy immutable tables and then to incremental maintenance under mutation. Linear's work trial is multi-stage by construction (kickoff → check-ins → demo) rather than multi-part in the puzzle sense.

### Cited Findings
- Notion `computeAggregations`: Part 1 compute row counts, sums, averages for a simulated Notion table; Part 2 immutable tables with many aggregation requests → lazy cache + cache-update logic; Part 3 mutable tables → fast aggregate updates via version-based snapshots and incremental maintenance. Interviewer "nodded throughout"; outcome not reported. — [programhelp.net, Nov 15 2025](https://programhelp.net/en/vo/notion-sde-interview-experience); corroborated in shape by "computeAggregations-style function ... then discuss efficiency as data grows" — [SupaCV](https://supacv.com/blog/interview-playbook/notion)
- Notion task-scheduler design round escalated through interviewer probes on queue design rationale, priority adjustment, and trigger mechanism. — [programhelp.net](https://programhelp.net/en/vo/notion-sde-interview-experience)
- Notion stubbed text editor: "complete several pre-stubbed functions" — inherently staged by function, but the function list is not public. — [linkjob.ai, Jan 14 2026](https://www.linkjob.ai/interview-questions/notion-software-engineer-interview-questions/)
- Notion claimed take-home → onsite extension: build simplified block editor (block types → drag-and-drop reordering → undo/redo), then later "extend your own take-home live" with a Notion engineer, testing "how maintainable and readable your original code was". Uncited. — [techprep.app Notion](https://www.techprep.app/blog/notion-interview-process)
- Linear work trial stages: kickoff (review prompt, context, initial approach) → candidate-led check-ins → final discussion-based presentation; project scoped backwards from "what a successful output looks like". — [Linear work trials post](https://linear.app/blog/why-and-how-we-do-work-trials-at-linear)

### Inferences
- Notion's escalation pattern (correct → efficient under scale → correct under mutation/concurrency) matches its product domain (tables/databases, collaborative editing) and the "run and test your code" screen format; a reasonable mock would be: implement aggregation API → add memoization keyed by table version → support incremental updates on cell edits.
- Supabase and Drata "parts" above are inferred by prep sites from the product domains (Postgres/RLS/CDC; integrations/evidence), not reported.

### Gaps
- No multi-part problem reports for Linear (none exist by design), Supabase, Drata, Phantom or Stack AV.

## Key Question 3: Format, time limits, AI-tool policy — do any allow or require AI coding tools?

### Takeaway
Notion is the only company with an official, explicit AI policy found: the 45-min technical screen **prohibits** ChatGPT/Gemini/Claude and "agentic coding tools" (documentation lookup allowed); claims that Notion runs an AI-tool-encouraged round are uncited prep-site assertions. Linear's trial is real-work in the company's tools, with no published AI rule. No AI policy was found for Supabase, Drata, Phantom or Stack AV.

### Cited Findings
- Notion technical screen: 5 min intro / 45 min problem / 5–10 min Q&A; CodeSignal IDE by default, own IDE allowed with notice; candidates asked to "refrain from using tools like ChatGPT, Gemini, Claude, or agentic coding tools"; looking up information permitted, Google AI Overview answers "fine when looking up questions or documentation". — [Notion: Technical Screen Preparation](https://notion.notion.site/Technical-Screen-Preparation-020644ad2c944fb2b9ae1c744e12b94d)
- Notion onsite: two 60-min coding interviews + 60-min system design to a rough spec; AI Applications Engineer practical is 60 min over Google Meet, run-and-test expected. — [Notion SWE Interviews](https://notion.notion.site/Software-Engineering-Interviews-63876cbf0aa9455fa0f0ffc854b40f6a); [Notion AI Applications Engineer](https://notion.notion.site/AI-Applications-Engineer-Interviews-2b7efdeead05807bb43de0444435812c)

## Key Question 4: Which companies use take-homes or paid work trials instead of live coding?

### Takeaway
Linear replaces coding interviews entirely with a paid 2–5 day work trial (official, 2026). Supabase reportedly uses a 4–6 hour unpaid take-home for senior/staff (third-party only). Notion is live-coding-first, with a take-home only claimed by prep sites. Phantom shows one report of a "home assignment". Drata and Stack AV show live rounds only.
