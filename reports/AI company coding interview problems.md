# AI company coding interview problems

**Question answered:** what multi-part (progressive, staged) coding problems have Decagon, Mercor, Reddit, Netflix, Block, Snowflake, Brex, Affirm, Snap, Coinbase, Linear, Notion, Drata, Supabase, Phantom, Stack AV, Glean and the highest-paying AI labs (OpenAI, Anthropic, Databricks, xAI, Scale AI, Cursor, Ramp, Figma, Harvey and others) asked software engineers between November 2025 and November 2026, in enough detail to turn them into timed, pytest-judged Solvary templates?

Research date: 2026-11-12. Six research notes (one per company group plus one cross-cutting) are in `research_notes/AI company coding interview problems/`; every claim below carries an inline source and one of these labels: **first-hand** (a candidate's own write-up), **official** (company-authored), **second-hand** (aggregator or prep-site synthesis of candidate reports), **reconstruction** (a prep site's rebuilt statement, or ours). Items dated before November 2025 are marked **OLD** and used only when they are the sole evidence for a pattern.

Two standing caveats shape the whole report. Glassdoor returned HTTP 403 to every fetch (snippets only), 1point3acres thread bodies are login-walled (titles and one-line summaries only), and several "candidate reports" are published by vendors of covert AI interview-assist tools (Linkjob, TechScreen, programhelp). Where a problem's parts rest only on such sources it is labelled accordingly. Sierra and Perplexity are already on Solvary (see `reports/Sierra coding interview problems.md`) and appear here only as comparison points.

## 1. Executive summary

The last year's loops at AI-adjacent companies have converged on one shape: a small stateful system whose requirements arrive in two to five waves on the same code, judged by tests, with part 1 deliberately easy and the pass line at part 3. Two formats carry it. The **CodeSignal Industry Coding Assessment** (ICA) is an asynchronous 90-minute, four-level (sometimes five or six) take-home with visible tests and unlock-on-pass, used in the window by Anthropic, OpenAI (some tracks), Coinbase, Ramp, Meta, Airbnb, Circle, xAI and Robinhood. The **live design-and-extend screen** is 45 to 60 minutes in CoderPad or a shared CodeSignal session where the interviewer adds parts verbally (Decagon, Snowflake, Reddit, Netflix, Snap, OpenAI, Databricks, xAI, Scale, Figma). Both reward a data model chosen in the first 10 to 20 minutes that absorbs timestamps and later mutations without a rewrite, regression-safe extension (earlier tests stay green), writing your own tests, and narration. Several first-hand reports passed without finishing the last part; several others were rejected after finishing everything on code quality or communication.

A third, newer shape matters for how Solvary is used rather than for its problem bank: **AI-assisted coding rounds** (Coinbase, Block, Snap, Netflix, Meta, DoorDash, Brex, Sierra) hand the candidate an existing multi-file codebase and a coding agent and grade decomposition, verification and the ability to explain every line. Section 5 covers them.

## 2. Company by company

### 2.1 Decagon

**Loop and format.** Recruiter screen → one or two ~45-minute CoderPad coding screens (typically two problems: a stateful class with escalating parts, then a counting or backtracking problem) → a "system design" screen that is really data modelling with scalability out of scope → onsite with pair coding, system design, project deep dive and a culture round that asks "a project, a project that went wrong, how you use AI". First-hand Sep 2026 and Jul 2026 reports and a New Grad Apr–Jun 2026 report ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-rejected-after-the-onsite-culture-screen), [PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-shopping-cart-versioning-then-rejected-on-tic-tac-toe), [FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/experience/2026-decagon-new-grad-swe-interviews-1-experience-4p0yzlfvh5)). No source states an AI-tool policy; language reported as Python or TypeScript (uncited). No report distinguishes the Agent Deployment Engineer, Senior SWE AI Platform or Senior SWE Product tracks.

**Problems with parts.**

- *Recipe shopping cart* (first-hand, Jul 2026, rated hard, rejected despite finishing): "You're building a shopping cart for a cooking app. Users add recipes to the cart, each recipe has a list of ingredients. When an ingredient appears in multiple recipes, the total count qualifies for bulk discounts." Part 1 `add_recipe`, `remove_recipe`, `get_total_discounts`. Part 2 `checkout(version)` and `get_version`: every cart operation creates a version; checking out an older version discards newer ones; the interviewer required an operations log rather than snapshots ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-shopping-cart-versioning-then-rejected-on-tic-tac-toe)). Undo/redo variant first-hand Sep 2026 and in a Jun 2026 summary ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-rejected-after-the-onsite-culture-screen), [1point3acres](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1180593)). Nested `begin/commit/abort` variant is a FastPrep reconstruction rated "about 90% match": discount queries see uncommitted changes, outer abort reverses inner commits, inner abort keeps earlier outer changes, depth ≤ 100 ([FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/problems/decagon-nested-recipe-cart-transactions)). Versioned-variant example from the same reconstruction: add A, get_version, add B, get_total_discount, checkout 1, get_version, get_total_discount → `[1, 3, 1, 0]`.
- *Rolling CSAT tracker* (first-hand, Apr 2026, 45 min, passed): `__init__(window_size)`, `record(conversation_id, score, timestamp)` with strictly increasing timestamps, `get_average(current_time)` rounded to 2 dp; follow-up 1 `update(conversation_id, score)` only while the conversation is in the window; follow-up 2 percentile query ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-csat-tracker-question-on-the-phone-screen)). PracHub's formal version: active iff `current_time - timestamp <= window_size`, `get_average` returns 0.0 when empty, nearest-rank percentile `ceil(p/100*n)-1`, window up to 1e9, 2e5 operations ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/coding-questions/implement-a-csat-sliding-window-tracker)). FastPrep counts 10 public reports through Nov 2026 ([FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/decagon-interview)).
- *Count legal tic-tac-toe games* (first-hand Jul 2026, SWE and "AI Engineer"): count distinct complete game sequences from an empty 3×3 board, X first; answer 255,168; the interviewer first asks for an order-of-magnitude estimate (9! upper bound). Variants: from a partial board (`["XX.","OO.","..."]` → 73), 4×4 with three-in-a-row wins ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-ai-engineer-interview-experience-a-tic-tac-toe-sequence-counting-problem-in-the-coding-round), [FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/problems/decagon-count-complete-tic-tac-toe-games)).
- *Batch API requests with retry delays* (second-hand, Sep 2026): calls accept at most 5 requests; part 1 minimise calls; part 2 failed requests carry a per-request wait, retry no earlier than allowed while others keep flowing, no busy-wait or blocking sleep; listed follow-ups: concurrency, client-wide rate limit, lost-response idempotency, deterministic tests ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/send-requests-in-batches-of-five-and-retry-failures-after-a-per-request-delay)).
- *Exclusive CPU time from nested logs* (LeetCode 636; 4 reports to Mar 2026) ([FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/decagon-interview)).
- *Design screens* (first-hand Oct 2026): a rule-management service for AI agents with drafts, publish and rollback; a delayed job scheduler; chatbot security; web crawler. Onsite: an "AI Gateway" in both the coding and the design round (multi-tenant, provider fallback, rate limiting, audit, quotas) ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/decagon-software-engineer-interview-experience-a-system-design-screen-that-was-really-data-modeling), [PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/design-an-ai-gateway)).

**What graders valued.** Space-efficiency unprompted (the ops-log requirement), stating time and memory costs while coding, catching your own bugs in tests. Finishing was not sufficient: the Jul 2026 candidate finished both problems and was rejected.

### 2.2 Mercor

**Loop and format.** ~20-minute background call → ~30-minute **spoken, no-code** technical screen with 4–5 rapid questions → role screen → in-person SF onsite, noon to 5 pm: coding ~90 min (60 solo, screen-recorded, then 30 live), a spoken algorithms round, a whiteboard system design (usually a job scheduler), and a 90-minute "candidate search" build with LLMs allowed (75 build, 15 discuss) against an evaluation endpoint. AI policy is round-specific and was inconsistently communicated (a pre-interview email banned LLMs; day-of links allowed them). Twenty-two reports synthesised by AceOffer, five first-hand screens on PracHub ([AceOffer](https://aceoffer.app/interviews/mercor/interview-process), [Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/hows-mercor-onsite-interview-like-r8sg0tnn)).

**Screen bank (small and recycled; the Oct 2026 candidate had seen almost all of it in prior posts).** Design the worst correct sorting algorithm and its complexity; two people each buy two coffees, probability one person's both rank above the other's (1/6); check-and-insert for 1M ids (set → Bloom filter → memtable); code review of a CSV builder (O(n²) string concatenation, trailing comma, escaping); code review of a charge-then-record payment method (idempotency key, write pending before the provider call, reconciliation); a threaded memoisation cache with double compute (lock scope) ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/mercor-software-engineer-interview-experience-technical-screen-with-bloom-filter-and-payment-idempotency-debugging), [PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/mercor-software-engineer-interview-experience-half-hour-online-round-of-code-review-and-design-questions)). Applied-AI screen (first-hand Sep 2026): debug an async Stripe refund function that marks the record done even when all three retries fail and has no idempotency key, then make it idempotent, plus rapid LLM questions (RAG vs fine-tune, context limits, prompt injection) ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-experiences/mercor-software-engineer-interview-experience-applied-ai-tech-screen-with-an-async-refund-debugging-question-and-rapid-fire-llm-questions)).

**Onsite coding.** A three-part "referral network" problem (parts 1–2 async with no LLM, part 3 live; graders want cycle-safe traversal, a clarified flow-centrality definition and your own tests) or a two-part task scheduler; exact statements not public ([AceOffer](https://aceoffer.app/interviews/mercor/interview-process)). Spoken algorithms: sort or top-k with a comparator that is wrong 10% of the time, deterministic per pair → minimise comparisons → minimise wall-clock with 5-second calls ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/sort-with-a-90percent-accurate-paid-comparator-algorithm-accuracy-and-cost)); k-hop friend network via a restricted `get_friends` API with the fewest calls → pagination cap and rate limit ([PracHub](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/exploring-a-friend-network-through-a-restricted-api-with-as-few-calls-as-possible)); currency conversion graph. MLE onsite (first-hand Sep 2026): parking-spot feasibility → DFS follow-up; "design a workflow orchestration system"; the room was double-booked mid-interview.

**Fit for Solvary.** Mercor's multi-part content is mostly spoken or async-plus-live, so it is a poor source of pytest templates. The friend-network-under-pagination problem is the only candidate, and it overlaps Sierra's product feed.

### 2.3 Glean

**Loop and format.** ~60-minute phone screen (one problem plus a short design question) → 4–5 round virtual or SF onsite: two ~45-minute coding rounds, a search-flavoured system design, a ~2-hour solo "on-the-spot build" in a local IDE, behaviour. The FDE posting adds "a brief AI-focused exercise or discussion"; MLE candidates report an AI-paired exercise. AI policy otherwise unreported. 1point3acres holds ~60 reports from 2026 and a 37-post summary, all login-walled ([1point3acres](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/company/glean), [techinterview.org](https://www.techinterview.org/post/3233476794/glean-engineering-interview/)).

**Visible 2026 screen problems (titles and one-line summaries).** OOD chess backend with bishop move validation and blocked paths (Nov 2026, fail); parallel document indexing (Oct 2026, pass, "limited time for additional test cases"); BPE tokenizer with train, encode and decode (Jun 2026, pass); Word Search I → II → follow-up (Apr 2026); kth largest across two sorted arrays; 2048 board for Senior Frontend (Oct 2026); Connect 4 (frontend, Sep 2026); a rate-limited Wikipedia crawler, AI-paired, MLE (Sep 2026) ([1point3acres](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1195961), [FrontendLead](https://frontendlead.com/discuss/t/glean-frontend-engineer-technical-phone-screen/3673)). The only concrete description of the 2-hour build is a Nov 2024 Blind comment (OLD): a multi-part use case such as pull data from an external system, transform it, store it in memory, query it; everything must run; judged on low-level design ([Blind](https://www.teamblind.com/post/glean-assignment-coding-round-rop1zkmg)).

**Fit for Solvary.** The chess and tokenizer screens are natural staged builds but the part boundaries are not recoverable; the tokenizer overlaps the Perplexity byte tokenizer. Low priority until a first-hand statement surfaces.

### 2.5 Netflix

**Problems with parts.**

## 3. Cross-company families and the CodeSignal format

### 3.1 The ICA skeleton

CodeSignal's own brief (2024, origin spec) defines one language-agnostic project in four progressive levels, a 90-minute cap, scores 200–600, partial credit per test, and per-level targets that deliberately exceed the cap: L1 "initial design and basic functions" 10–15 minutes and 15–20 lines; L2 "data structures and data processing" 20–30 minutes, 30–45 lines cumulative; L3 "refactoring and encapsulation" 30–60 minutes, 90–130 lines; L4 "extending design and functionality" 30–60 minutes, 110–160 lines total. "Please do not change the existing method signatures." Complex algorithms, concurrency and third-party libraries are excluded by design ([CodeSignal brief](https://discover.codesignal.com/rs/659-AFH-023/images/Industry-Coding-Skills-Evaluation-Framework-CodeSignal-Skills-Evaluation-Lab-Short.pdf)). Every in-window ICA variant shares one shape: L1 CRUD with explicit failure values; L2 a ranked or filtered report with a deterministic tiebreak; L3 time enters (timestamps on every op, TTL, scheduled or held transactions processed lazily before each later op); L4 a state-rewriting operation that must respect everything before it (rollback, restore, merge, version rollback, overdue report). Companies using it in the window: Anthropic, Coinbase, Ramp, Meta, Airbnb, Circle, Robinhood, eBay, Instacart, xAI (five levels).

The L3 traps named by first-hand reports are where to aim pytest cases: an expired transfer must refund its source; an accepted transfer must count toward activity; due scheduled payments must run before any later operation; the operation at exactly the expiry timestamp; a rejected operation must leave state unchanged ("validate before mutating"). The community mock for cloud storage shows tests and prose disagreeing on whether rollback is destructive, so pin that in any statement ([GitHub issue](https://github.com/PaulLockett/CodeSignal_Practice_Industry_Coding_Framework/issues/16)).

### 3.2 Recurring families

| Family | Where seen in the window | Typical escalation |
|---|---|---|
| In-memory KV / database | Anthropic, OpenAI, Coinbase, xAI, Snowflake, Netflix (versioned), Databricks (snapshot iterator) | CRUD → scans/prefix → timestamps + TTL → backup/transactions/persistence |
| Cache with eviction | Netflix, Snap, Anthropic, Databricks, xAI, Harvey, Figma | LRU → TTL → non-scanning cleanup → thread safety → callbacks/crash resilience |
| Ledgers and credits | OpenAI, Coinbase/Meta/Airbnb banking, Anthropic, Ramp, Databricks referral credits | balances → ranked report → expiry/scheduled payments → merge/out-of-order replay |
| Rate limiters and sliding windows | Snowflake, xAI, OpenAI, Ramp, Databricks, Decagon CSAT, Coinbase moving average, Reddit abuse detection | fixed → tiered/runtime-updatable → tests with injected clock → distributed |
| Rule-accreting simulations and games | OpenAI infection, Anthropic threshold infection, Databricks tic-tac-toe, Decagon tic-tac-toe count, Scale card game, Reddit tennis | base rules → new state types → timed transitions → interventions |
| Task queues and schedulers | OpenAI, Scale, Anthropic OA, xAI, Ramp, Mercor, Netflix/Databricks design | CRUD → dependencies → deadlines/cooldowns → DLQ/at-least-once |
| Trees and hierarchies | Reddit comment filter and moderator logs, Snowflake multiway tree, Notion blocks | filter → aggregate (post-order) → mutation under invariants |
| Concurrency on top | Anthropic crawler and image pipeline, Databricks logger, Netflix playlist, Snap LRU | single-threaded → bounded workers → lock placement → "interviewer keeps adding conditions" |

### 3.3 Pass lines

Fully passing level 3 with earlier tests still green is the de facto pass line for 90-minute ICA problems (coaching claim: ~520/600 at Anthropic; Airbnb 440 presumed fail). For live rounds, OpenAI's "first 3 of 5 with clean BFS", Reddit's "parts 4–5 are the staff signal", and several candidates who passed with a bug in the last part all point the same way. Rejections after finishing cite non-concise code (Netflix), a design that stored snapshots instead of operations (Decagon), and unclear narration.

## 4. What this means for Solvary templates

- **Add the ICA format as a new template kind.** A 90-minute `levels` interview with four parts, tests visible per part, next part unlocked on green (Solvary's `on_accept` already does this), and per-test partial credit mapped onto a 200–600 score would be a faithful mock of the format used by Anthropic, Coinbase, Ramp, Meta and Airbnb. Shortlist items 1, 2 and 9 are its first three problems. Realistic pacing targets: L1 by 15, L2 by 35, L3 by 75 minutes.
- **The live design-and-extend screen is already Solvary's shape** (Sierra set, 30-minute budgets). Shortlist items 3–8 and 10–12 fit it directly; items 5 and 6 should end with a part that is a verbal follow-up in a real interview (distributed, concurrency) which Solvary can only approximate with tests that inject a clock or check invariants.
- **Overlap check before adding.** Item 8 (expiring credits) against the Perplexity credit tracker; item 15 against Sierra product feed; the ICA task manager against the Perplexity todo tracker; Glean's BPE against the Perplexity byte tokenizer; OpenAI's spreadsheet against the Sierra spreadsheet.
- **The interviewer agent is the right place for the AI-era signals.** Narration, clarifying the I/O format before coding, and "write your own tests in the last 5–10 minutes" are what reports say separates candidates, and they are only measurable with an interviewer in the room.

## 5. AI-assisted coding rounds

The named AI-allowed rounds in the window: Meta (60-minute CoderPad with a built-in assistant and selectable models, multi-file project, pilot Oct 2025, 2026 rollout), DoorDash (60-minute session on your own machine with Cursor, Claude Code or Codex, Mar 2026), Google (Gemini in a "code comprehension" round, H2 2026 pilot), Sierra (Plan → 2-hour Build with any tools → Review, Apr 2026), Canva (since Jun 2025), Coinbase (AI-assisted pre-screen since Mar 2026 and a four-part repo round; AI fluency rated for every candidate), Block (Kotlin "Live AI Build", Aug 2026), Snap (official "AI Assisted Coding" round on HackerRank), Netflix (confirmed in one L5 onsite, content undescribed), Brex (the onsite project requires agentic coding), Anthropic (performance take-home only; a pilot elsewhere), OpenAI (beta agentic round). Cursor's and Linear's work trials are the extreme form ([interviewing.io](https://interviewing.io/blog/how-to-use-ai-in-meta-s-ai-assisted-coding-interview-with-real-prompts-and-examples), [Sierra](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview), [Canva](https://canva.dev/blog/engineering/yes-you-can-use-ai-in-our-interviews)). Industry-wide the share is still small: Interview Query estimated 1.1% of technical interview reports in Q3 2026 described an AI-assisted round ([Interview Query](https://www.interviewquery.com/p/ai-in-technical-interviews-2026-data)).

When AI is allowed the problem changes shape: a multi-file starter codebase, a feature of 120+ lines or an ambiguous product task, 60–120 minutes, and grading on decomposition before prompting, specific prompts, small verified increments, catching the assistant's bugs, and being able to explain every line. Fail signals reported by Meta, Block, Coinbase and Canva interviewers: accepting output without understanding, letting the assistant design the whole solution, one-shotting the whole task. The no-AI camp (Anthropic and OpenAI live rounds, Databricks, Vercel, Notion, Perplexity) compensates with standard-library-only environments, no autocomplete, and time pressure on exactly the multi-part problems above. For Solvary this suggests a later template kind: a small starter package with existing tests plus a staged feature list, run with the interviewer agent watching the process.

## 6. Gaps and caveats

- **Source access.** Glassdoor (all mirrors), the Coinbase and DoorDash blogs, the Snowflake careers blog, CodeSignal's rules page, several Medium debriefs and Interview Query pages returned 403 or 429; 1point3acres bodies and PracHub "Hard" entries are paywalled. Problem statements from those are snippets or aggregator reconstructions and are labelled so.
- **Vendor-authored "reports".** Linkjob, TechScreen and programhelp publish candidate accounts while selling covert AI interview assistants; their process facts are usable but their incentives are not neutral. Techinterview.org's company guides are uncited and contradict official pages at Linear and Anthropic.
- **Role-specific loops.** No report at all for: Decagon's three named roles, Mercor's three, Reddit's three, Netflix Agent Platform and Productivity, Block Developer and Employee Experience, Snowflake Senior AI Engineer and Frontend AI Product (beyond two items), Brex Workbench, Affirm Backend AI Platform, Snap Full Stack L5 Los Angeles, Coinbase FDE Finance, Agentic Trading and ML CX, Stack AV in the window, Phantom's AI Engineer. The closest proxies are the general SWE loops at each company.
- **No dedicated prompting, agent-design or evals round was found anywhere.** "AI rounds" are software-engineering rounds with an agent in the loop; the only LLM-engineering content is Harvey's RAG notebook and eval-design follow-ups, Reddit's BigQuery-agent design, Figma's eval-platform design, and Mercor's rapid LLM questions.
- **Unknown statements.** Mercor's three-part referral network, Scale's card-game rules, Glean's chess and tokenizer part boundaries, Block's Live AI Build task, Brex's agentic project, OpenAI's agentic round, Anthropic's OA stages 5–6, Snowflake's logger-library parts.
- **Frequency claims** ("very high frequency", "28 reports") come from paywalled aggregators and cannot be independently verified.
- Compensation figures are Levels.fyi self-reported medians read on 2026-11-12 and prep-site estimates; they are included only to justify "pays a lot".
