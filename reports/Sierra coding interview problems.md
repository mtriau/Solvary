# Sierra's interviews trade LeetCode for agent plumbing

Sierra's engineering coding rounds are dominated by one multi-part, practical exercise: fetch from a flaky or paginated API, transform the response (resolve fallback product IDs, build a list, merge/dedupe records), then compact a conversation history to a token budget, all in roughly 30 minutes of a 45–60-minute CoderPad screen with no AI allowed. At least nine independent first-hand reports from November 2025 to October 2026 describe that family, and most candidates finish only two of its three parts ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [1point3acres, Oct 2026](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1193376)). Around it sit a smaller set of corroborated problems: header-aware Markdown chunking (April–May 2026), spreadsheet circular-reference detection (2024–2025, the one genuinely LeetCode-style screen), a "Debug View" interval merge/overlap OA (November 2025), a planted-bug debugging round on a 4–5-file Python agent (or a TypeScript/React app) judged against a flowchart with customer-impact follow-ups, and a take-home to build a customer-service agent for a fictional outdoors company by picking 2 of 5 features and writing up production metrics. Sierra officially replaced its coding phone screen with system design and its coding/algorithms onsites with a Plan → 2-hour AI-assisted Build → Review session on 22 April 2026, but every dated candidate report after that still shows the no-AI practical coding screen running on the Software Engineer, Agent / Agent Engineer / AI Engineer and new-grad tracks, so a candidate should prepare for both loops ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). Python and TypeScript are the only accepted languages, and Python-only candidates have been refused a switch in the TypeScript/React debugging round. The full problem catalog, with statements, follow-ups, dates, source counts and confidence labels, is in the section "Every reported Sierra problem, grouped by round".

## Two loops coexist: the practical loop survives the April 2026 redesign on agent and new-grad tracks

Sierra's own account of its old loop is "two coding interviews, plus interviews for algorithms, system design, and culture fit, followed by reference checks," preceded by a coding phone screen written "without AI in an online editor"; the authors concluded the signal was "mostly about mechanics, such as typing syntax, recalling algorithms, and assembling frameworks," and that hiring managers "leaned more on referrals and prior experience when the interview signal was unclear" ([Sierra blog, 22 Apr 2026](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). Candidate reports from 2024 through early 2026 describe something more specific than that generic description, and it is the loop a candidate is most likely to actually meet on the agent-engineering track: a **30-minute recruiter call** that asks for a language preference (Python or TypeScript), a **45–60-minute multi-part practical screen on CoderPad**, a **take-home agent build** with a provided OpenAI API key, and an **in-person onsite of three roughly 60-minute rounds**: a debugging round on starter code with planted bugs, a presentation and live extension of the take-home, and a hiring-manager behavioral round on agency and collaboration ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc)). The Gaijineer account places the debugging round as a second remote CoderPad session before the onsite; the Blind and Aced accounts place it inside the onsite. Early-career tracks add an online assessment and a product-design round: the APX (L3 rotational) loop was a 1-hour live Python technical with an agent product engineer, then an in-person pair of rounds (flowchart-driven debugging, and product design with the head of product), and the October 2026 new-grad loop ran OA → next-day invite → 45-minute pair-programming phone screen → "project-style problem" ([Aced, APX](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22); [1point3acres 1192600](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192600); [1point3acres 1192604](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192604)).

On 22 April 2026 Vijay Iyengar, Arya Asemanfar and Angie Wang published "The AI-native interview," and Bret Taylor amplified it the same day ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview); [Bret Taylor on X](https://x.com/btaylor/status/2046996207898366248)). The redesign has three components. The coding phone screen became a **system design interview** "focused on taking work to production at scale," on the reasoning that "vibe-coding an app is easy" and the harder problem is "getting it into production in a scalable way." The coding and algorithms onsites were removed and replaced by an **AI-native onsite**: **Plan**, a working session in which the candidate drives ideation for a product "in their own domain" while interviewers probe to strengthen it; **Build**, where the interviewer leaves and the candidate builds for **2 hours "using the AI tooling and frameworks of their choice,"** free to pivot or cut scope, with advance guidance that it is "OK to cut your scope as you build, and to skip boilerplate (CRUD, auth) to focus on what's unique"; and **Review**, a demo followed by discussion of product flows, a code review of data model, abstractions and extensibility, the path to production, and "how they used AI along the way." Evaluation criteria are shared with candidates in advance, are "agnostic to what the candidate builds," and interviews run in pairs; the named signals are agency (pivoting when stuck), judgment (scoping within time), and technical judgment in code review ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). The third component is a **debugging interview still in pilot**: "a medium-sized codebase and a colleague's draft PR that adds a cross-cutting feature," which the candidate reviews and improves by "pulling down the code, inspecting output, and iterating with coding agents," with "the level of AI used in this interview still TBD" because new models zero-shot many fixes. Infrastructure roles get the same onsite "slightly amended," with no public detail on the amendment. Sierra cites two example builds: a trivia enthusiast's "AI-powered game meant to keep the user in a state of flow," and a backend engineer's "headless simulation tool" demoed by "an agent with a markdown file" ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). Durations for Plan and Review, whether candidates bring their own laptop, and the content of any actual system-design prompt are not published anywhere, and no candidate has written up the new onsite from the inside; the only hints are an onsite teaser from July–September 2026 listing "a customer service chat system, pub/sub, error handling, debugging, and AI coding exercises" and a March 2026 Blind commenter who had "just finished their phone interview" and wanted "insight on the system design interview" ([1point3acres 1192971](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192971); [Blind, Mar 2026](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-onsite-3l31zi3c)).

The decisive finding for a candidate is that the redesign did not retire the practical coding screen. Every dated first-hand report after 22 April 2026 still describes a live, no-AI coding round: a Software Engineer phone screen on Markdown chunking in April 2026, a 45-minute Agent Engineering phone screen in June 2026, a Senior AI Engineer (L5) pagination screen in spring 2026, an AI Engineer "chatbot compaction" screen around September 2026, a Staff Agent Engineer "paginated list + merge" screen in mid/late 2026, and both a new-grad phone screen and an AI-Agent video interview on pagination and token compaction in October 2026 ([Screna](https://www.screna.ai/experience/1b782b07-a73a-4631-b69b-fcf9461ac00f); [1point3acres 1181173](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1181173); [Aced, L5](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-senior-ai-engineer-interview-087f4e); [Aced question 7216](https://www.aced.io/questions/7216/implement-chatbot-compaction-logic); [FrontendLead](https://frontendlead.com/discuss/t/sierra-agent-engineer-experience/3624); [1point3acres 1193376](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1193376); [1point3acres 1192645](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192645)). The take-home question "Build your own customer service AI agent for a hypothetical outdoors company" was still being logged as asked around June 2026 ([Aced questions](https://www.aced.io/questions?company=sierra-ai)). The reconciliation is that the April post was written by platform/research engineering leads about the core experienced-SWE loop, while the customer-facing "Software Engineer, Agent" track (Sierra's forward-deployed title, posted in SF, NY, Atlanta, London and a dozen other cities), the AI/Agent Engineer roles, and the early-career pipeline continue to run the take-home-plus-practical-screen loop ([sierra.ai/careers](https://sierra.ai/careers); [Aced guide](https://www.aced.io/guides/sierra-agent-engineer-interview)). Sierra has not said whether the Plan/Build/Review onsite will eventually replace the agent-track take-home. A candidate for a core Platform Engineering role should expect the system-design screen and the 2-hour build; a candidate for an Agent/AI Engineer or new-grad role should expect the practical coding screen, the take-home and the debugging round, and should confirm with the recruiter, who by every account explains the full process upfront.

## Tools, languages and AI policy split cleanly by round

The live tool is **CoderPad**, "sometimes with screen sharing," for both the technical screen and the agent-codebase debugging round, which is run in the CoderPad terminal; no report mentions HackerRank or CodeSignal, and the new-grad OA platform is unidentified ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/hiring-insights/09aca2a9-2474-40bb-960a-e0b7f4c76de8)). "Sierra primarily works in Python and TypeScript, and they want you to interview in one of those," and the recruiter asks the preference on the first call; the first-hand advice is that "if you're a Java or C++ person, you'll want to get comfortable in Python or TS before the process starts" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience)). The language promise has a hard exception: the Agent Engineer onsite debugging round was "prewritten TypeScript and React code," the Python-strongest candidate asked to switch and was refused, and that candidate "pretty much bombed" and attributes the rejection to it, warning "don't trust the 'language-agnostic' framing" ([Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc)). The take-home is built against an OpenAI API key with tool calling, and interviewers reward a loop that calls tools directly without an agent framework, which is a guide inference from candidates who did exactly that rather than a stated Sierra rule ([Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc); [vibeengines](https://vibeengines.com/handbook/fde-interview-sierra)). Interviews are in person at a Sierra office (SF or NY for US candidates), with travel paid ([sierra.ai/careers](https://sierra.ai/careers); [Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v)).

AI policy is the clearest line between rounds. In the live screen and the classic debugging round, **no LLMs**: the APX candidate reports "LLMs were not allowed, but looking up Python syntax was," the October 2026 new-grad screen ran "with no AI tools allowed," the Aced guide says the debugging round "historically ran without AI coding tools," and Sierra itself describes the old screen as written "without AI in an online editor" ([Aced, APX](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22); [1point3acres 1193376](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1193376); [Aced guide](https://www.aced.io/guides/sierra-agent-engineer-interview); [Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). For the take-home, **AI is encouraged**: the December 2025 Blind candidate was told "you are encouraged to use Cursor for it," the only source naming a specific tool ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v)). For the new 2-hour Build, AI is **required in practice and itself evaluated**: "the AI tooling and frameworks of their choice," with Review digging into "how they used AI along the way" ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview)). For the draft-PR debugging pilot the policy is explicitly undecided. The practical consequence for the take-home is that AI use is fine but ownership is tested: the onsite presentation is "many 'why' questions, such as why you chose a given architecture or approach to tool calling," interviewers have already run the submitted code before the session, and the candidate is asked to add a feature live ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc); [Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v)).

## Thirty minutes for three parts: pacing, not algorithms, is what fails candidates

Round lengths are consistent across reports: recruiter 30 minutes (60 in one Agent Engineer loop), screen 45–60 minutes, onsite rounds 60 minutes each, Build 2 hours, and whole loops of 2–6 weeks with the technical screen sometimes a month after the recruiter call ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc); [Aced guide](https://www.aced.io/guides/sierra-agent-engineer-interview)). The number that matters is inside the screen: one candidate's 45-minute slot was explicitly "coding for 30 minutes and leave 15 min for my questions," a Telegram re-post describes "three parts, with only 30 minutes for coding," and the two most recent reports both say the third part was left unfinished ("coding round didn't have enough time for 3rd round but finished explanation"; "a third coding task that was left unfinished") ([Blind alter post](https://www.teamblind.com/alter/evanescer-4frjdcpc); [t.me/usinterview](https://t.me/s/usinterview?q=%23sierra); [Aced question 7216](https://www.aced.io/questions/7216/implement-chatbot-compaction-logic); [1point3acres 1192645](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192645)). The screen is deliberately over-scoped, and finishing two of three parts is survivable: the December 2025 Blind candidate "solved 2 of 3 parts" and advanced to the onsite ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v)). Budget about ten to fifteen minutes per part and expect to narrate part three rather than code it.

Where time goes is also consistent: not into algorithms but into I/O plumbing. "Most of the time went to getting the code to run and parsing the data," and the November 2025 screen failure was "calling an API getting json array and creating a linked list," which the candidate says "wasn't hard" but where "you have to know your language well (types etc.) to make sure Json parsing is not artificial making you stuck" ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [Blind, Nov 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-interview-process-z4clm77u)). Difficulty is rated LeetCode easy-to-medium per part by the people who passed, and Glassdoor's aggregates put the whole company at about 3.2/5 and Agent Engineer at 3.8/5 (7 reviews, 75% positive, ~18 days to hire), visible only via snippets because every Glassdoor domain returned 403 ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [Glassdoor Agent Engineer](https://www.glassdoor.com/Interview/Sierra-Agent-Engineer-Interview-Questions-EI_IE10032095.0,6_KO7,21.htm)). The "practical, not LeetCode" character is the single most repeated general statement: "Not LeetCode, it simulates a real scenario you'd encounter working at Sierra," "each follow-up adds new requirements on top of your existing code, testing whether you write extensible code from the start," "it's not really leetcode," and "prep less for classic LeetCode and more for hands-on debugging and reading unfamiliar code" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [Aced, APX](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22)). The counter-evidence is older or vaguer: the 2024–2025 Agent Engineer screen was "LeetCode-style" cycle detection with the advice "prepare graph and tree LeetCode problems," a March 2026 Blind commenter called the screen "LC hard for me (one of the easier/well-known ones though)," and Glassdoor snippets mention "a standard DSA LC question" ([Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc); [Blind, Mar 2026](https://www.teamblind.com/post/has-anyone-heard-of-sierra-ai-kxr183qo); [Glassdoor SWE](https://www.glassdoor.co.in/Interview/Sierra-Software-Engineer-Interview-Questions-EI_IE10032095.0,6_KO7,24.htm)). Date explains most of the disagreement: 2024–early 2025 screens leaned LeetCode, late 2025–2026 screens are practical multi-part, and the April 2026 core loop has no algorithms round at all.

Debugging rounds have the same over-scoping shape. There are more planted bugs than most candidates find in 60 minutes (3 in the agent codebase, ~4 found of more in the APX round "before time ran out," 5–6 in the TypeScript/React app), and the scoring is as much about the narrated method and the customer-impact answer as the fix: "how each bug would affect the customer experience and why it would be harmful to users" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Aced, APX](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22); [Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc)). The hiring-manager round, not any coding round, is the most-cited failure point among candidates who passed the technical rounds: the December 2025 Blind candidate "passed all rounds except this one," found the hiring manager "rude" about a non-CS background, and the Gaijineer account warns "this isn't a generic 'tell me about a time' round. The questions are specific and they dig into details" ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience)). Behavioral questions are anchored in Sierra's five values (Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity, Family), and "they really do abide by their values" ([sierra.ai/about](https://sierra.ai/about); [Aced, FDE](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-forward-deployed-engineer-interview-99c71f)).

### Phone screen and live technical problems

**S1. Paginated or flaky API fetch, then transform, then compact to a token budget.** Confidence: **multiple first-hand reports** (nine independent candidates, November 2025 to October 2026, across Software Engineer-Agent, AI Engineer, Senior AI Engineer L5, Staff Agent Engineer and new-grad tracks). The fullest first-hand statement is Gaijineer's: part 1, call an API endpoint that fails intermittently but eventually succeeds, so implement retry until success; part 2, the response contains a product list where products carry fallback IDs; part 3, iterate the list and resolve each product's eventual set of IDs into product objects, with "each follow-up adding new requirements on top of your existing code" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience)). The December 2025 variant used a client Sierra provides: "the first part connects to an API using a provided client. The second and third parts transform the response object, roughly LeetCode Easy and Medium" ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v)). The November 2025 variant was "calling an API getting json array and creating a linked list" from a mock API ([Blind, Nov 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-interview-process-z4clm77u)). The Senior AI Engineer (L5, spring 2026) variant was "implement pagination for an API to retrieve all results, then do some computation with the JSON response" ([Aced, L5](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-senior-ai-engineer-interview-087f4e)). The Staff Agent Engineer variant was "building a paginated list + processing it to merge items," with the advice to handle "fetching every page (cursor/offset) cleanly before the merge step" and to clarify "how duplicates should be merged" ([FrontendLead](https://frontendlead.com/discuss/t/sierra-agent-engineer-experience/3624)). The 2026 third part is LLM-flavored: "implement chatbot compaction logic" (AI Engineer, ~September 2026), "API fetching, token-limit data compaction, and a third coding task that was left unfinished" (AI-Agent video interview, October 2026), and "a conversation agent, pagination, and token-based compaction" with no AI tools (new grad, October 2026) ([Aced question 7216](https://www.aced.io/questions/7216/implement-chatbot-compaction-logic); [1point3acres 1192645](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192645); [1point3acres 1193376](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1193376)). A Telegram re-post adds "part 1 was simple: repeatedly fetch data from an API until no more data is returned," three parts, 30 minutes of coding, two finished ([t.me/usinterview](https://t.me/s/usinterview?q=%23sierra)).

Two PracHub reconstructions (October 2026, community question bank) give the fullest concrete spec and match the teasers. Variant A: `fetch_all(fetch_data)` where `fetch_data(cursor)` returns a batch of records (each with a non-negative integer `tokens`) plus the next cursor, first call `cursor=None`, empty batch ends the stream, return all records in order; discuss a repeated cursor, an API that never returns empty, transient failures without losing or duplicating records, and memory versus streaming. Then `compact(records, trigger=20_000, target=10_000)`: if total tokens exceed the trigger, return the longest contiguous run (original order) whose total fits the target, else return unchanged; worked example `[4000, 3000, 5000, 2000, 1000, 6000, 3000]` totals 24,000, no run of four fits, runs of three such as `[3000, 5000, 2000]` fit. Follow-ups: maintain the budget incrementally as records are appended, always keep the most recent record, maximize tokens instead of count, summarize excluded records ([PracHub, fetch/compact](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/fetch-every-page-from-a-data-api-then-compact-records-to-a-token-budget)). Variant B: "you are building the message-history layer of a conversation agent"; frozen `Message` dataclass (`id, role, text, tokens`); `fetch_page(cursor)` returns a page plus next cursor or `None`. Part 1, assemble one ordered list across pages with a clear stop condition, handling empty pages, transient failures, misbehaving/repeated cursors, eager versus lazy. Part 2, when total tokens exceed the limit, drop the oldest messages until it fits, cheap enough to run after every message (running total, O(1) front removal via deque), with edge cases for a single oversized message, empty history, and must-keep messages. Follow-ups: summarize old messages and budget the summary's tokens, keep tool calls and their results together, avoid duplicates or gaps when new messages arrive mid-pagination, test without a live service; the page notes "the exercise has more parts than the two described" ([PracHub, conversation history](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/conversation-agent-history-paginated-fetching-then-token-limit-compaction)). FastPrep's title-only catalog ("Compact Conversations by Unique Listings," "Compact Conversation History by Token Budget," "Paginate Retained Conversation History," "Maximum Array Value," "Markdown Header Chunks") corroborates the family and suggests a dedupe-by-unique-listing variant ([FastPrep](https://www.fastprep.io/hiring-insights/09aca2a9-2474-40bb-960a-e0b7f4c76de8)).

**S2. Split a Markdown document into header-aware chunks under a size limit.** Confidence: **multiple first-hand reports** (Screna, Software Engineer technical phone screen, April 2026; 1point3acres, Software Engineer, April–June 2026 quarter, posted 15 May 2026; a Telegram re-post echoes it). Statement: "Given a markdown file, split it into chunks that each respect a maximum size limit. When a chunk boundary falls within content under one or more headers, the new chunk must repeat those ancestor headers before its content." The candidate rated it "Intermediate," found base chunking manageable, second-guessed the header-prepending step, and says the edge cases were the interesting part ([Screna](https://www.screna.ai/experience/1b782b07-a73a-4631-b69b-fcf9461ac00f); [1point3acres 1176834](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1176834)). PracHub's formalization (rated Hard, updated 19 May 2026): `split_markdown(markdown: str, max_size: int) -> list[str]`; a header is one to six `#` plus a space at column 0; a level-L header clears active headers at levels L through 6; chunks are packed greedily; if a chunk starts with a header line, repeat only its parent headers; size counts the final string including inserted headers and newlines; `("", 10) -> []`; `("# Title\nHello\n## Section\nWorld", 100)` returns one unchanged chunk; input up to 200,000 characters, and any single line plus its header context fits in `max_size` ([PracHub, Markdown](https://prachub.com/interview-questions/split-markdown-into-header-aware-chunks)).

## What to practice: timed three-part Python drills built around Sierra's own job

The evidence points to a narrow practice set, and the first-hand advice is unusually consistent: "practice practical coding, class design, and writing extensible code that handles changing requirements," "API calls, error handling, retries, and pagination, plus JSON data wrangling," and "build an agent without hiding behind a big framework, understand OpenAI tool calling, and be ready to discuss metrics, observability, and feature trade-offs" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience); [Aced, L5](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-senior-ai-engineer-interview-087f4e); [Aced, Agent Engineer](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-machine-learning-engineer-interview-8549fc)). For timed multi-part Python practice, the highest-yield drill is S1 in all its variants under a 30-minute clock: a cursor-paginated fetch with retry and backoff that neither drops nor duplicates records, then a transform (resolve fallback IDs to objects, dedupe by a key, build a linked list from a JSON array), then a token-budget compaction (drop-oldest with a deque and a running total, or longest contiguous run with a sliding window), then the follow-ups on incremental maintenance, must-keep messages, and summarizing the dropped tail. The time sink in every account is I/O and typing rather than algorithms, so the drill should start from a mock client that returns real JSON with awkward types, and part boundaries should be rehearsed as additions to existing code rather than rewrites, because "each new requirement is added on top of your existing code, testing whether you write extensible code from the start" ([Gaijineer](https://gaijineer.co/sierra-software-engineer-agent-interview-experience)). The second tier is S2 (header-stack Markdown chunker with greedy packing and size accounting that includes the inserted headers), O1 (interval merge with text concatenation, then overlap/gap flags), S3 (dependency graph with cycle detection, including a 2D-grid DFS form), S5 (cycle-safe catalog tree traversal), and S8 (an undo/redo class whose requirements expand mid-problem). The third tier is reading unfamiliar code: take a 4–5-file tool-calling agent loop, plant three logic bugs and one prompt or tool-calling bug, and practice locating them from a flowchart and CLI output while narrating the customer impact of each, because the APX advice is to "practice explaining the customer impact of bugs, not just the fix" ([Aced, APX](https://www.aced.io/experiences/sierra-ai-interview-67fe22)). Anyone on the Agent Engineer track must also be able to debug a small TypeScript/React app without switching languages.

For the take-home, build the T1 agent end-to-end at least once before the process starts: a raw OpenAI tool-calling loop with two concrete customer-service features (order status, returns, booking changes), a short design note, and a metrics/observability section covering containment, escalation, latency, tool-call error rates and conversation-level evals, and then rehearse adding a third feature live in under 30 minutes. For the April 2026 core-SWE loop, rehearse a 2-hour build of a product you already understand, with scope cut aggressively and boilerplate skipped, and keep a log of how AI tools were used, since Review asks about it; the system-design screen should be prepared around taking an LLM-backed customer-service chat system to production (pub/sub, retries, failure handling, observability), which is the only reported post-redesign onsite theme ([Sierra blog](https://sierra.ai/blog/the-ai-native-interview); [1point3acres 1192971](https://www.1point3acres.com/interview/thread/1192971)). Behavioral preparation should produce specific, detailed stories mapped to Trust, Customer Obsession, Craftsmanship, Intensity and Family, since the hiring-manager round is where candidates who clear every technical round still fall ([Blind, Dec 2025](https://www.teamblind.com/post/sierra-ai-hiring-and-future-peb5ne2v); [sierra.ai/about](https://sierra.ai/about)).

## Conclusion

The striking pattern in Sierra's coding rounds is that they are drawn from the agent engineer's actual workday rather than from an algorithms syllabus: retrying a flaky customer API, resolving a product catalog, fitting a conversation into a context window, chunking documents for retrieval, merging timed logs for a debug view, and reading a tool-calling loop that misbehaves. The problem bank is small and stable, with the same three-part API screen reported by senior, staff, new-grad and agent-track candidates across a full year, which makes targeted rehearsal unusually effective, and the scoring emphasis (pacing through parts, extensible code, narrated trade-offs, customer impact) rewards practice under a clock more than breadth of algorithms.

The open question is how far the April 2026 redesign will spread. Today it governs the core Platform Engineering loop on paper while the Agent, AI Engineer and early-career tracks keep the no-AI practical screen, the Cursor-friendly take-home and the planted-bug debugging round, with the draft-PR pilot and its undecided AI policy sitting between them. The direction is unmistakable, toward evaluating judgment with AI tools rather than typing without them, so a candidate preparing now should treat the practical three-part screen as the floor and a defensible, scope-cut, AI-assisted build as the ceiling, and ask the recruiter which of the two loops their requisition runs.
